## What it is

On Earth the rescuer's body weight does the work of chest compressions. In weightlessness there is no weight to push with, so the rescuer must hold on to the patient.

## What Hygieia shows

The emergency screen shows one method by default, the Evetts-Russomano method: the rescuer's legs are wrapped around the patient's torso, the arms are locked, and compressions come from the upper body. A metronome runs at 110 beats per minute.

## Alternatives

Two other methods are described for microgravity. In the handstand method the rescuer braces their feet against the opposite wall and pushes down on the patient's chest. In the reverse bear hug the rescuer holds the patient from behind and compresses the chest with both arms. These are different methods; the Evetts-Russomano method is not the handstand method.

## In partial gravity

In the Lunar base and Mars surface profiles the screen shows kneeling CPR instead.

## Limits

Hygieia's triage trees stand in for procedures a flight surgeon would approve.
